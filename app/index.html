How does this little thing work? ↗

Each flash marks a recorded spike from this simulation. The view shows a selection of active neurons, arranged by their distance from the sensory inputs. Lines are real connections from the selected fly network. The layout is a schematic, not a reconstruction of the brain’s shape.

Signals on a line show a firing neuron influencing a connected neuron; they do not prove which input made that next neuron fire. The digit is computed from the completed simulation and revealed when the replay ends.

The network contains 30,000 simulated neurons. Its connections stay fixed; a trained reader uses activity from 6,000 neurons to predict a digit. Sample recordings come from the training dataset.

This model’s benchmark · speakers excluded from training

The noisy test uses artificial background noise. These scores do not measure your laptop mic or EarPods; those need separate recordings. The displayed samples were used to train the final demo reader.

A separate speech check rejects quiet or non-speech recordings before prediction. It can miss quiet speech or accept some noises. The saved benchmark measures the digit reader, before this check.
